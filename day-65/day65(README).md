Theory-only day (Angela Yu's course, Web Design School unit). No coding exercise —

## Color Theory
- **Hue, saturation, value** - hue is the base color (red, blue, etc.), saturation is how intense/pure it is, value is how light or dark it is.
- **Warm vs. cool colors** - warm colors (red, orange, yellow) feel energetic, urgent, and tend to pop/advance visually - good for CTAs and drawing attention. Cool colors (blue, green, purple) feel calm and trustworthy, and tend to recede - good for backgrounds.
- **Color psychology** - specific colors carry associations (red = energy/urgency/danger, blue = trust/calm, green = growth/nature). Pick a palette based on the mood the site needs to convey, not personal preference.
- **Tints and shades** - adding white to a color makes a tint (softer), adding black makes a shade (deeper). Useful for building a consistent palette from one base color instead of picking random unrelated colors.
- **Complementary colors** - colors opposite each other on the color wheel create high contrast and are useful for making one element (like a button) stand out against the rest of the page.

## Typography
- Text should either be **consistent** (same font family throughout) or **intentionally contrasting** (e.g. a distinct display font for headings + a clean, readable font for body text) - never an inconsistent mix that looks accidental.
- Typography isn't just decoration - it guides the user's eye and affects readability directly, which affects how long people stay on the page.

## Layout & Alignment
- Elements should follow a consistent grid/alignment - misaligned elements make even good content look unpolished.
- Alignment creates implicit visual relationships between elements (grouping related things together).

## Whitespace
- The single most emphasized point from the lecture: don't cram elements together. Whitespace isn't wasted space - it gives content room to breathe and directs attention to what matters.

## Visual Hierarchy
- Use size, weight, and position to signal importance - the most important element (headline, primary CTA) should be the first thing the eye lands on.

## Understanding Your Audience
- Design choices (palette, tone, density of information) should be driven by who is actually going to use the site - not by the designer's personal taste.
